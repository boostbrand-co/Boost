# STRATEGY SUMMARY

## The position

**Marketing you can actually watch work.**

BOOST is a marketing partner for established businesses in the US and Mexico. Strategy, creative and
paid media run as one system, with every decision written down and every account owned by the client.

The position is verifiability, not capability. Everyone in the category claims results, data and
partnership. Almost nobody can be checked. The audit of roughly 130 marketing-services providers
found five of the original whitespace hypotheses collapsed on contact, and what survived with a
documented zero was always a mechanism rather than a claim.

## Why this and not the alternatives

| Considered | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| AI-native agency | Around 8 in 10 agency sites already claim AI-powered. Buyers now read it as a discount trigger, and roughly a third of agencies have already been asked for an AI discount |
| Creative growth agency | The current position. Both halves are on the saturation list, and it describes a category rather than a difference |
| Bilingual agency | Language is table stakes and depreciating as AI translation improves. The defensible version is native execution and cross-border operating leverage |
| Performance guarantees | Buyers said plainly that whoever defines the result controls the guarantee. Guarantees now read as downmarket at this tier |
| Full-service | The word codes down-market in the competitive set, and the specialist economics run against it |

## The four mechanisms that carry it

1. **Named operators before you sign.** "Who will actually be doing the work on my account?" was the
   most-repeated question in the buyer research, and almost no agency site answers it.
2. **Client ownership from day one.** Accounts, pixels, analytics, domain, source files, historical
   data, decision log and Creative Memory. Cited as a deal-breaker in five or more threads and
   published as a policy by nearly nobody.
3. **Reporting that ends in a decision.** Six questions, not a hundred metrics, with a published
   misses section. The most consistent complaint in the corpus was reports that never connect to the
   business.
4. **A written protocol for missing.** Including the point at which BOOST recommends ending the
   engagement. Zero of roughly 130 providers publish one.

## The compounding argument

Creative Memory is the thesis. Every agency claims marketing that gets smarter; none ships a
client-visible record of what each test taught. The site publishes the record structure, states that
it is an operating methodology rather than proprietary software, and commits that it leaves with the
client. This is also the weakest-served pattern across the 80-site interaction study, where only two
service businesses handled compounding value explicitly.

## Who it is for

Established SMB and mid-market businesses with real budget, in five situations: replacing an agency,
supporting an in-house team, a growth plateau, a launch or reposition, and US-Mexico growth. The
first two are commercially the most valuable because almost no competitor addresses them, and
replacing an agency is the most common documented purchase trigger.

## Who it is not for

Published on `/fit`: pre-product-market-fit companies, budgets too small for testing to signal,
cheap-posting buyers, anyone wanting guaranteed results, anyone unwilling to share revenue data, and
single-task work. Stating this is a qualification device, and it was the strongest trust pattern in
the research.

## The service architecture, and what it implies

Five groups: strategy and direction, creative and content, growth and acquisition, web and
conversion, measurement and intelligence. Strategy and measurement bracket the loop and carry the
premium. Production sits inside as a supporting discipline, which is a deliberate response to the
AI threat map: content, social posting, reporting, basic SEO, small builds and media-buying execution
are all commoditising, while creative strategy, measurement architecture, allocation judgment and
accountability are appreciating.

## What the website is doing commercially

It answers the questions buyers ask before they book, so the first conversation starts further along.
Gartner's own data cuts against the naive reading of rep-free preference: buyers are 1.8 times more
likely to complete a high-quality deal when digital tools and a human work together, and fewer than a
quarter of rep-free preferrers say it is about avoiding salespeople. The fix is a site that answers
things, not a site that removes the human.
