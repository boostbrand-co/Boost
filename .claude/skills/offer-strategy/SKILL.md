# Offer Strategy

Use this skill when defining services, packages, product ladders, pricing structure, flagship offers, entry offers, upsells, qualification, or offer-market fit.

## Principle

An offer is the commercial packaging of value. It should make it easy for the right buyer to understand what they get, why it matters, what it requires, and what the next step is.

Do not confuse an offer with a list of deliverables.

## 1. Business context

Clarify:

- business model
- delivery capacity
- margins / cost structure when available
- team constraints
- revenue objective
- target market
- existing clients
- sales cycle
- proof available

## 2. ICP by buying situation

Define the best-fit buyer through:

- company / person type
- trigger
- current problem
- urgency
- budget logic
- decision-maker
- current alternative
- buying objection
- desired outcome

Avoid generic demographic ICPs when buying context matters more.

## 3. Value architecture

Separate:

- outcome
- mechanism
- deliverables
- proof
- support
- speed
- risk reduction
- customer responsibilities

Lead with the outcome and mechanism. Deliverables support the promise.

## 4. Offer ladder

Map:

- free / awareness
- entry offer
- core / flagship offer
- premium / expansion
- recurring / retention

Not every business needs every level.

## 5. Scope

Define:

- included
- excluded
- assumptions
- dependencies
- number of revisions / rounds
- channels
- cadence
- ownership
- client responsibilities

Scope should protect delivery quality and margin.

## 6. Pricing logic

When pricing data exists, evaluate:

- cost to deliver
- target margin
- buyer value
- competitive frame
- complexity
- risk
- commitment length
- payment cadence

Do not invent prices without enough context. Use ranges or decision logic when exact pricing is pending.

## 7. Qualification

Decide whether the offer should be:

- self-serve
- application / qualification
- sales call
- custom proposal

High-ticket or operationally complex offers may need fit screening even when the client wants low friction.

## 8. Proof

Attach proof to the promise:

- case studies
- quantified outcomes
- before/after
- process artifacts
- testimonials
- credentials
- guarantees / policies if real

Do not use unrelated proof just because the number is impressive.

## 9. Positioning the offer

Define:

- one-line outcome
- who it is for
- what makes the mechanism different
- why now
- proof
- next step

Avoid inflated names that make a simple service harder to understand.

## 10. Conversion path

Map the path from traffic source to purchase / sales call.

Make sure the landing page, sales message, pricing, and CTA all describe the same offer.

## Acceptance checklist

- Is the best-fit buyer clear?
- Does the offer lead with outcome rather than tasks?
- Is scope protected?
- Is pricing logic coherent?
- Is the sales path appropriate for complexity?
- Does proof support the main promise?
- Can the team deliver this consistently and profitably?

If not, revise before marketing it.
