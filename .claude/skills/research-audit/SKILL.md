# Research Audit

Use this skill for client audits, competitive research, website reviews, market research, evidence reviews, offer analysis, funnel audits, and strategic discovery.

## Principle

Research should reduce uncertainty and improve decisions.

Do not produce a long document of observations without separating evidence, interpretation, risk, and action.

## 1. Define the question

Before collecting information, clarify:

- decision the research should support
- scope
- geography
- audience
- timeframe
- channels / assets included
- source constraints
- what is already known

## 2. Source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order when available:

1. first-party business data
2. first-party customer / product evidence
3. official / primary external sources
4. direct competitor materials
5. reputable secondary analysis
6. community / qualitative discussion
7. inference

Label inference clearly.

## 3. Evidence ledger

For every material claim capture:

- claim
- exact evidence
- source
- date / timeframe
- confidence
- whether it is fact, interpretation, target, or assumption
- contradiction, if any

Never silently reconcile conflicting numbers or versions.

## 4. Audit dimensions

Choose only those relevant to the task.

### Business / offer
- category
- offer architecture
- pricing
- value proposition
- product ladder
- friction
- proof
- retention / repeat logic

### Audience
- jobs
- triggers
- objections
- language
- decision criteria
- alternatives

### Brand
- positioning
- differentiation
- voice
- visual conventions
- trust
- category fit

### Website / funnel
- information architecture
- hero clarity
- navigation
- offer visibility
- CTA path
- friction
- mobile
- conversion proof
- forms / checkout
- trust signals

### Content / social
- themes
- consistency
- proof
- audience fit
- format mix
- hooks
- CTA behavior
- comment / community signals

### Paid acquisition
- creative patterns
- offer
- landing-page continuity
- proof
- audience-message fit
- performance evidence

### Competitors
- category framing
- offer
- price
- proof
- channel strategy
- visual territory
- claims
- whitespace

## 5. Competitor analysis

Do not stop at screenshots or feature tables.

For each competitor identify:

- who they target
- what they promise
- how they monetize
- strongest proof
- primary acquisition angle
- category language
- visual territory
- weakness / tradeoff

Then summarize saturated territory versus credible whitespace.

## 6. Customer voice

When reviews, comments, calls, messages, or search discussions exist, extract:

- repeated phrases
- desired outcomes
- complaints
- objections
- moments of surprise
- comparison language
- purchase triggers

Preserve exact wording where useful. Do not turn every observation into a generic “pain point.”

## 7. Contradictions

Create a visible contradiction list.

Examples:

- pricing differs across sources
- website and sales deck describe different offers
- targets presented as results
- different launch dates
- inconsistent naming
- old and new packages coexist

Treat contradictions as decisions to resolve, not details to smooth over.

## 8. Findings

Each finding should include:

- evidence
- interpretation
- business consequence
- recommendation
- priority

Avoid findings such as “the website could be clearer.” Name exactly what is unclear and what decision it blocks.

## 9. Prioritization

Use a simple hierarchy:

### Critical
Blocks trust, conversion, product truth, legal safety, measurement, or launch.

### High
Meaningfully affects growth, positioning, acquisition, or customer experience.

### Medium
Improves quality but does not block progress.

### Low
Polish.

## 10. Recommendations

Recommendations should be executable.

Prefer:

- change X to Y
- move section A before B
- remove unsupported claim
- create landing page for audience Z
- split one offer into two entry paths
- add proof artifact at decision point

Avoid generic advice such as “improve branding” or “post consistently.”

## 11. Final output

A strong audit usually contains:

- executive readout
- what is working
- what is not working
- evidence / contradiction log
- audience / competitor insights
- prioritized opportunities
- concrete next actions
- questions that still require confirmation

## Acceptance checklist

- Is every important conclusion grounded?
- Are facts separated from inference?
- Are contradictions visible?
- Does the audit explain business consequence?
- Are recommendations specific enough to execute?
- Are priorities clear?
- Did the research answer the original decision question?

If not, keep working.
