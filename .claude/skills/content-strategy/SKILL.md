# Content Strategy

Use this skill for social strategy, editorial planning, campaign systems, founder content, brand content, email content, or multi-channel content planning.

## Principle

Content should move a business objective and build a recognizable point of view.

Do not start with “content pillars” until audience behavior, business goals, and proof are understood.

## 1. Inputs

Review:

- business objective
- offer / conversion path
- audience jobs and objections
- brand strategy
- verbal identity
- proof available
- channel performance
- existing content
- production capacity

## 2. Define the job of content

Choose the primary jobs:

- awareness
- category education
- trust
- demand generation
- conversion support
- retention
- community
- founder authority
- product education

Avoid asking every post to do everything.

## 3. Audience situations

Organize content around moments such as:

- problem recognition
- comparison
- objection
- aspiration
- decision
- onboarding
- success / proof

This usually produces stronger content than broad topics alone.

## 4. Content territories

Create 3–6 durable territories.

Each territory should define:

- audience job
- business purpose
- point of view
- proof source
- useful formats
- CTA behavior

Territories should be specific enough that competitors cannot use the same labels without changing them.

## 5. Proof system

Build recurring proof formats:

- case metrics
- screenshots
- before / after
- process artifacts
- customer language
- behind-the-scenes decisions
- product demo
- expert commentary

Do not let the feed become claims without evidence.

## 6. Format architecture

Use multiple repeatable formats:

- short-form video
- carousel
- static proof
- founder POV
- case story
- FAQ / objection
- product walkthrough
- long-form post
- email

Define which formats are native to each channel.

## 7. Hook standard

Hooks should start from a real tension, observation, number, situation, or opinion.

Avoid generic hooks such as:

- “3 things you need to know” without a real reason
- “stop scrolling”
- vague contrarian setups
- clickbait that the content cannot pay off

## 8. CTA system

Map CTA to funnel stage:

- save / share
- comment / reply
- visit
- compare
- book
- buy
- join
- request

Use low-friction CTAs when the audience is early. Do not force sales CTAs into every post.

## 9. Production system

Build around real capacity.

Define:

- monthly planning cadence
- research inputs
- batch production
- approval stages
- asset requirements
- repurposing
- publishing
- reporting

Content strategy should survive the team’s actual workflow.

## 10. Measurement

Choose metrics based on the content job.

Examples:

- reach / qualified views
- watch time
- saves / shares
- profile visits
- clicks
- leads
- conversations
- conversion
- assisted revenue

Do not treat vanity metrics as business outcomes.

## Acceptance checklist

- Does each territory serve a real audience job?
- Does content connect to an offer or business objective?
- Is proof built into the system?
- Are formats varied but recognizable?
- Are hooks specific rather than generic?
- Is the production cadence realistic?
- Are metrics matched to the content job?

If not, revise.
