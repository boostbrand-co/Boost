# Stop Slop 2

Use this skill whenever writing, rewriting, editing, or reviewing prose that should sound human, natural, specific, and professional.

This is BOOST's extended version of Hardik Pandya's public **Stop Slop** framework. It keeps the anti-AI-writing rules that make the original effective, then adds BOOST's professional application rules and a dedicated **Message Mode** for Slack, WhatsApp, email, DMs, texts, and client communication.

Source: https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop
License of source framework: MIT.

## 1. Core objective

Remove predictable AI tells without flattening the writer's real voice.

The goal is not casualness. The goal is writing that sounds like a capable human with something specific to say.

Protect:

- meaning
- factual accuracy
- technical precision
- personality
- market-appropriate language
- useful nuance

Cut:

- filler
- generic emphasis
- formulaic structure
- performative sincerity
- narrator-from-a-distance voice
- over-explanation
- AI cadence

## 2. Core Stop Slop rules

### Cut filler phrases

Start with the point. Remove throat-clearing such as:

- "Here's the thing"
- "Here's why"
- "The truth is"
- "The reality is"
- "At its core"
- "It's worth noting"
- "Let me be clear"
- "At the end of the day"
- "When it comes to"
- "In today's..."

Remove emphasis crutches such as:

- "Full stop"
- "Period"
- "Let that sink in"
- "Make no mistake"
- "This matters because" when the next sentence can simply state why

### Break formulaic structures

Avoid predictable AI pivots:

- "Not X, but Y"
- "X isn't the problem. Y is."
- "The answer isn't X. It's Y."
- "It feels like X. It's actually Y."
- "The question isn't X. It's Y."
- "Not just X, but also Y"
- negative buildup before the actual point

State the useful point directly.

### Avoid dramatic fragmentation

Do not manufacture importance with fragments:

- "X. That's it."
- "And Y. And Z."
- one-word revelation lines
- repeated punchy endings

Use complete sentences unless the speaker's real voice naturally uses a fragment.

### Use active voice

Name the actor.

Prefer:

"The team changed the offer."

over:

"The offer was changed."

Avoid giving inanimate things fake agency when a person or company is responsible.

### Be specific

Replace vague significance with the actual fact.

Weak:
"The implications are significant."

Better:
"This adds a second approval step and pushes launch by one week."

Use names, actions, numbers, dates, constraints, examples, and conditions when they matter.

### Put the reader in the room

Avoid abstract narrator voice such as:

- "People tend to..."
- "Nobody designed this..."
- "This happens because..."

Use the actual actor, audience, or situation.

### Vary rhythm

Mix sentence lengths and paragraph shapes.

Avoid:

- three consecutive sentences with the same cadence
- every paragraph ending in a punchline
- three-item lists by default
- constant rhetorical questions
- excessive em dashes

Prefer commas, periods, colons, or sentence restructuring.

### Trust the reader

Do not explain the same point twice.
Do not justify every recommendation.
Do not soften clear statements without a reason.
Do not summarize what the reader just said before answering unless acknowledgment is useful.

### Cut engineered quotables

If a sentence sounds written mainly to become a pull quote, rewrite it into a useful statement.

## 3. Professional application

Stop Slop 2 should make professional writing more human, not less precise.

Keep useful domain language when it carries real meaning, including terms such as:

- CAC
- MRR
- ARPU
- GTM
- ROAS
- conversion
- positioning
- attribution
- scope
- deliverables

Do not replace precise terminology with vague plain-English substitutes just to sound casual.

Use complete explanations when the reader needs context.

Professional does not mean stiff. Human does not mean sloppy.

## 4. Adverbs and modifiers

The original Stop Slop framework uses an aggressive rule against adverbs because AI overuses them.

BOOST applies that as a strong filter, not a blind ban.

Remove adverbs when they only intensify or soften:

- really
- just
- literally
- genuinely
- honestly
- simply
- actually
- deeply
- truly
- fundamentally
- inherently
- importantly
- crucially

Keep one only when it changes the factual meaning or matches the speaker's natural voice.

## 5. Business-language cleanup

Replace inflated business language when a simpler verb says the same thing.

Prefer:

- handle instead of navigate challenges
- explain instead of unpack
- revisit instead of circle back
- increase instead of double down
- analysis instead of deep dive when appropriate

Do not strip useful industry terminology.

## 6. Message Mode

Trigger Message Mode whenever the output is a message, reply, Slack note, WhatsApp, text, DM, email response, follow-up, approval request, update, or short interpersonal communication.

Message Mode adds these rules on top of the universal writing standard.

### Start with the answer or action

If someone asked a question, answer it first.

If you need something, ask for it clearly.

If you are confirming a decision, confirm it without a long preamble.

### Do not repeat the recipient

Avoid paraphrasing what they just told you before responding.

Weak:
"Thanks for letting me know that you want slides 4, 6 and 8 combined..."

Better:
"Agreed. I'll combine 4, 6 and 8 and keep the visual idea from 6."

### Match the channel

Slack / WhatsApp / DM:

- short
- conversational
- direct
- minimal greeting
- minimal sign-off

Email:

- enough context to stand alone
- clear structure
- explicit next step

Formal or legal-adjacent:

- precise
- documented
- less casual

### Preserve the speaker's real voice

Use contractions when natural.
Keep the user's normal level of warmth, humor, directness, and punctuation.
Do not make every message sound like polished corporate copy.

### Avoid message-specific AI tells

Cut:

- "Thanks for sharing"
- "I completely understand"
- formulaic empathy
- repeated apologies
- customer-support tone
- overexplaining the recommendation
- long context before the actual ask
- unnecessary greetings and closings

### One message, one job

Know what the message needs to accomplish:

- get a decision
- get information
- confirm scope
- explain a recommendation
- update progress
- set a boundary
- schedule something
- follow up

Write toward that action.

### Disagreement

Do not make the other person defend their idea.

Useful pattern:

1. acknowledge the intent briefly
2. name the real risk
3. recommend placement or alternative

Do not turn disagreement into a mini essay unless the stakes require it.

## 7. Drafting workflow

### Pass 1: Meaning

Confirm the draft says the correct thing.

### Pass 2: AI tells

Remove throat-clearing, formulaic contrasts, fake emphasis, passive voice, vague declarations, and manufactured punchlines.

### Pass 3: Specificity

Replace vague language with names, actions, numbers, examples, evidence, conditions, or consequences.

### Pass 4: Voice

Make the wording sound like the actual speaker in the actual context.

### Pass 5: Compression

Cut anything that does not change meaning, tone, trust, or action.

### Pass 6: Medium check

If it is a message, apply Message Mode.
If it is a deck, report, website, proposal, or strategy document, keep enough context for asynchronous reading.

## 8. Quick checks

Before delivering prose, ask:

- Does the first sentence start with the point?
- Is the actor clear?
- Is there passive voice hiding responsibility?
- Is an inanimate thing doing a human action?
- Is there a "not X, but Y" pattern that can be stated directly?
- Is there a throat-clearing opener?
- Is any adjective or adverb doing the job of missing evidence?
- Did I repeat what the reader just said?
- Are three sentences using the same rhythm?
- Does every paragraph end with a dramatic one-liner?
- Is there a vague significance claim?
- Is any sentence trying too hard to be quotable?
- Could a competent human send or publish this exactly as written?

If not, revise.

## 9. Message Mode quick checks

For messages specifically:

- Did I answer before explaining?
- Is the ask or next step obvious?
- Did I avoid repeating the recipient?
- Does the length match the channel?
- Does it sound like a person rather than client-service software?
- Could I cut 20% without losing tone or meaning?

If yes, cut it.

## 10. Scoring

Rate 1–10 on:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Directness | Does it state the point instead of announcing it? |
| Rhythm | Does it sound varied rather than metronomic? |
| Trust | Does it respect the reader's intelligence? |
| Authenticity | Does it sound like a real person in this context? |
| Density | Is anything still cuttable? |

Below 35/50: revise.

## 11. Boundaries

Do not alter facts to make prose cleaner.
Do not remove necessary caveats.
Do not simplify technical language past accuracy.
Do not make formal writing casual when the situation requires precision.
Do not force every writer into the same voice.

The skill removes AI patterns. It does not erase personality.
