# Investor-Grade Presentations

This is a specialized extension of the universal `presentation-design` skill.

Use `presentation-design` as the default standard for every deck. Use this skill only when the audience is investors, financing partners, board members evaluating capital allocation, or diligence reviewers.

## Investor-specific priorities

Add these requirements on top of the universal presentation standard:

- Separate traction, targets, management scenarios, and modeled outcomes.
- Never turn negotiations into partnerships or targets into traction.
- Make the use of capital explicit.
- Tie every financing event to an operating proof point.
- Show why the opportunity can become large without presenting future valuation as fact.
- Use market sizing only when assumptions are defined and internally consistent.
- Make the team slide answer “why this team can execute.”
- Use founder or agency case studies only when they prove a capability relevant to the investment thesis.
- Prefer a tight live investor deck with a diligence appendix or leave-behind when necessary.
- Do not use hype to compensate for pre-launch status. Use clarity, proof, timing, operating logic, and disciplined ambition.

## Typical investor narrative

A useful pattern is:

**Problem → Why now → Thesis → Solution → Category gap → How it works → Business model → Market → GTM → Readiness / traction → Defensibility → Team → Execution proof → Validation → Financial scale → Ask → Roadmap → Close**

Do not force this order if a different sequence makes the investment case clearer.

## Investor acceptance test

Before finalizing, confirm:

- Can the investor explain the company after the first few slides?
- Is the business model obvious?
- Are market assumptions defensible?
- Is the current company stage represented accurately?
- Is the team’s execution credibility visible?
- Is the round tied to measurable proof?
- Does the financial story show scale without pretending certainty?
- Does the deck distinguish the live story from diligence detail?
- Does the close make the next conversation feel worth having?

When these conditions are met, return to the universal `presentation-design` skill for final visual and narrative QA.
