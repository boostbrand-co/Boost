# Verbal Identity

Use this skill after the business and brand strategy are sufficiently defined. The verbal identity translates positioning into repeatable language.

## Principle

A voice system should make different writers sound like the same brand without making every sentence sound identical.

Do not build voice from adjective lists alone. Define observable writing behavior.

## Inputs

Review:

- category definition
- positioning
- audience jobs and tensions
- product truth
- sales process
- customer language / VoC
- existing high-performing copy
- claims that are approved / restricted

## 1. Voice thesis

Define the brand voice in one behavioral sentence.

Example structure:

“The brand sounds like a person who understands both sides, explains what matters, and gives the user enough context to decide.”

## 2. Personality traits

Use 3–5 traits maximum.

For each trait define:

- what it means
- how it appears in copy
- how it appears in product/service
- what it should never become
- one before/after example

## 3. Tone sliders

Calibrate useful tensions:

- human ↔ technical
- warm ↔ formal
- practical ↔ aspirational
- calm ↔ energetic
- accessible ↔ premium
- selective ↔ universal

Use numbers only as internal calibration, not as customer-facing claims.

## 4. Tone by moment

Voice stays stable; tone adapts.

Define guidance for:

- discovery / homepage
- conversion
- onboarding
- support
- errors
- sales
- investor / PR
- social
- email
- product UI

For each, state what the copy must accomplish and what emotional state the reader may be in.

## 5. Voice rules

Write 5–10 concrete rules.

Examples:

- explain the category before using analogy
- use concrete verbs
- name the user’s next action
- use data before adjectives
- explain match/fit rather than promising perfection
- separate facts from estimates
- keep control language explicit where users retain control

## 6. Lexicon

Build three lists:

### Use more
Words central to the category and customer job.

### Use carefully
Words that may be useful but easily become vague or misleading.

### Avoid
Clichés, overclaims, jargon, or language that pulls the brand into the wrong category.

## 7. Naming conventions

Define preferred names for:

- users
- product actions
- plans / tiers
- stages
- buttons
- features
- support states

Prefer customer-language labels over internal jargon.

## 8. Messaging blocks

Create reusable working copy for:

- category descriptor
- master headline
- audience-specific promise
- proof line
- process line
- differentiator
- CTA

These are modules, not mandatory slogans.

## 9. UX copy system

For key states define:

- empty state
- error
- confirmation
- save
- contact
- onboarding prompt
- warning
- final step

Microcopy should state:

1. what happened
2. what the user can do
3. what the brand will do next, when relevant

## 10. Professional prose filter

Apply the `stop-slop-2` skill.

Use its universal mode for brand copy and its Message Mode when writing replies, DMs, email responses, Slack, WhatsApp, or other interpersonal communication.

Avoid:

- formulaic contrast
- exaggerated adjectives
- throat-clearing
- generic startup jargon
- dramatic fragments
- excessive em dashes
- fake conversationality

## 11. Examples

Include approved examples across multiple lengths:

- headline
- subhead
- paragraph
- CTA
- email
- social post
- support response

Examples should demonstrate the rules rather than replace them.

## Acceptance checklist

- Can two writers use this guide and produce recognizably related work?
- Are the rules behavioral and specific?
- Does the voice support the positioning?
- Does the lexicon reinforce category ownership?
- Are tone changes mapped to real moments?
- Are overclaims and product-truth boundaries reflected?
- Does the writing sound human, professional, and specific?

If not, refine the system.
