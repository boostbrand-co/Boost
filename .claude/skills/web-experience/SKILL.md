# Web Experience

Use this skill for websites, landing pages, product marketing sites, one-pagers, conversion flows, and digital brand experiences.

## Principle

A website should make the business easier to understand and easier to act on.

Do not treat the site as a stack of attractive sections. Build a decision path.

## 1. Define the job of the site

Clarify:

- primary audience
- primary conversion
- secondary conversions
- business model
- product / offer structure
- proof available
- traffic sources
- stage of awareness

## 2. Information architecture

Organize around user questions.

Typical sequence:

1. What is this?
2. Is it for me?
3. What result can I expect?
4. Why should I trust it?
5. How does it work?
6. What does it cost / require?
7. What should I do next?

Do not force every site into the same order. Preserve the buying logic.

## 3. Homepage discipline

The hero should communicate:

- category or job
- primary outcome
- audience
- next action

Avoid vague positioning that requires scrolling before the visitor knows what the company does.

## 4. Offer architecture

If multiple services / products exist, make hierarchy explicit.

Define:

- flagship offer
- entry offers
- supporting offers
- upsell / cross-sell
- which offers can self-serve
- which require qualification

Do not hide the highest-value offer three screens deep.

## 5. Conversion paths

Map each traffic source to the right next step.

Examples:

- Google high intent → product/service landing page
- social awareness → proof / education → CTA
- referral → credibility / case study → contact
- existing customer → expansion / booking

Avoid one generic CTA for every audience and stage.

## 6. Proof placement

Use proof near the decision it supports.

Proof types:

- quantified case study
- testimonial
- logo
- product screenshot
- process artifact
- before/after
- credential
- guarantee / policy

Do not put all proof in one “testimonials” section and expect it to carry the entire site.

## 7. Product truth

Do not imply capabilities the business does not provide.

Examples:

- booking
- payment
- verification
- guarantees
- live inventory
- instant response
- service coverage

If product capabilities are illustrative, label them internally and keep external copy accurate.

## 8. Visual storytelling

Use the active design system.

The page should alternate visual modes when appropriate:

- photography
- product UI
- data / proof
- process diagram
- editorial copy
- testimonial

Avoid endless identical cards.

## 9. Motion

Use motion to:

- clarify sequence
- maintain context
- reveal state
- connect related elements
- create hierarchy

Avoid motion that delays reading or exists only for spectacle.

## 10. Mobile

Mobile is not a compressed desktop.

Check:

- first-screen clarity
- thumb reach
- CTA persistence
- readable type
- form behavior
- horizontal overflow
- image crop
- section order

## 11. SEO / GEO readiness

Where relevant, include:

- clear category language
- page-specific intent
- descriptive headings
- structured FAQs
- schema
- internal links
- factual structured content

Do not write for search engines at the expense of human clarity.

## 12. Analytics plan

Define:

- pageview
- CTA click
- form start
- form complete
- checkout / booking
- source attribution
- scroll or content engagement only when useful

Measurement should map to decisions.

## Acceptance checklist

- Can the right visitor understand the offer in seconds?
- Is the highest-value path obvious?
- Does proof appear near key decisions?
- Are multiple audiences separated when needed?
- Does every CTA lead somewhere real?
- Is product truth accurate?
- Does the site have visual rhythm?
- Does mobile preserve hierarchy?
- Can performance be measured?

If not, revise.
