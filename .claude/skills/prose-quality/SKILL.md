# Prose Quality

Use this skill for any client-facing, strategic, website, presentation, email, report, proposal, caption, or internal prose that must sound human and professionally written.

## Standard

Write like an experienced operator communicating with an intelligent reader.

The goal is not casualness. The goal is clarity, specificity, rhythm, and trust.

## Core rules

1. State the point directly. Remove throat-clearing and announcement phrases.
2. Use active voice. Name the person, team, company, or user doing the action.
3. Prefer specific nouns, verbs, numbers, examples, and constraints over vague claims.
4. Remove filler, empty adverbs, generic emphasis, and corporate padding.
5. Avoid formulaic reversals such as “not X, but Y” when the point can be stated directly.
6. Avoid dramatic fragmentation and manufactured one-line profundity.
7. Vary sentence length and paragraph shape. Do not write in a metronomic AI rhythm.
8. Trust the reader. Do not over-explain obvious implications.
9. Preserve precise professional vocabulary when it carries meaning. Do not dumb down useful terms.
10. Keep the speaker’s actual voice, market, audience, and medium in mind.

## Avoid

- “here’s the thing”
- “the reality is”
- “at its core”
- “it’s worth noting”
- “game-changing”
- “unlock”
- “reimagine”
- “revolutionize”
- “world-class”
- “frictionless”
- “seamless” when the process has real steps
- excessive em dashes
- generic “future of X” framing
- vague phrases such as “the implications are significant”
- fake intimacy or performative honesty
- repeating back what the other person just said before answering

## Professional Stop-Slop filter

Use Hardik Pandya’s Stop Slop principles as a quality filter:

- cut filler
- break formulaic structures
- use active voice
- be specific
- put the reader in the room
- vary rhythm
- trust readers
- cut engineered quotables

Apply them selectively. Professional writing may still use technical terms, complete explanations, and formal structure when the audience needs them.

## Editing workflow

### Pass 1 — Meaning
Confirm the draft says the correct thing.

### Pass 2 — Specificity
Replace vague words with names, numbers, actions, examples, conditions, or evidence.

### Pass 3 — Structure
Remove repeated points, fake contrasts, and paragraphs that announce what the next paragraph will say.

### Pass 4 — Voice
Make the wording sound like a capable human in the relevant context.

### Pass 5 — Compression
Cut anything that does not change meaning, tone, trust, or action.

## Final checks

- Does the first sentence start with the point?
- Is the actor clear?
- Is any adjective doing the job of missing evidence?
- Did the draft repeat the recipient’s wording instead of advancing the conversation?
- Are there formulaic AI patterns?
- Does the rhythm vary naturally?
- Could a competent professional send this as written?

If not, revise.
