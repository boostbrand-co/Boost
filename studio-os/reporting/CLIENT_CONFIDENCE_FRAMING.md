# BOOST Client Confidence Framing

Status: V1 working standard  
Owner: BOOST  
Applies to: all recurring client performance reports and monthly review decks.

## Purpose

BOOST reports should be truthful, useful and confidence-building.

A client report is not an internal audit memo. It should show the real numbers, explain what changed, give context, and make the next action feel controlled.

The goal is **constructive framing**, not positive spin.

Do not hide material declines, invent explanations, or call weak results strong. Also do not give a contained issue more emotional weight than the overall account health deserves.

## Core rule

The emotional tone of the report should match the actual health of the account.

When the overall period is healthy and one channel, week, format or initiative softens:

1. lead with the verified progress,
2. name the softer result plainly,
3. add relevant context without overclaiming causality,
4. show the recovery / optimization plan,
5. avoid repeating the same negative signal across multiple slides.

When performance is materially weak across the account, say so clearly. Constructive framing must never become concealment.

## Executive-summary framing

The executive summary should establish the overall period before listing optimization areas.

Prefer:

- verified growth or progress first,
- the strongest business / brand result,
- one to three clear next-period focuses.

Do not create an equal positive-vs-negative scorecard by formula.

If four metrics improved and one contained area declined, the report should not feel like a crisis report because the decline receives four separate warning treatments.

### Preferred labels

Use labels such as:

- `LO QUE AVANZÓ`
- `FOCOS DEL SIGUIENTE PERIODO`
- `LECTURA BOOST`
- `DECISIÓN`
- `QUÉ SEGUIMOS CUIDANDO`

Use `LO QUE HAY QUE ATENDER`, `RIESGO`, `ALERTA`, `PROBLEMA`, or similar language only when the severity genuinely warrants it.

## Language calibration

Prefer calm, specific language.

Examples:

Prefer:
`Instagram mantuvo crecimiento en el acumulado, pero agosto bajó de ritmo después del hueco de publicaciones.`

Over:
`Instagram perdió fuerza.`

Prefer:
`Plan Estudiante todavía está por debajo de la referencia; vamos a probar una ejecución más fuerte antes de sacar conclusiones.`

Over:
`Plan Estudiante no conecta.`

Prefer:
`La caída coincide con una menor continuidad de publicación. Recuperaremos la cadencia y mediremos si vuelve el nivel anterior.`

Over:
`Instagram castiga los huecos.`

Prefer:
`El anuncio actual sigue resolviendo bien; vamos a renovar creativos para mantener el rendimiento.`

Over:
`La cuenta depende peligrosamente de un solo anuncio.`

## Context before concern

When a decline has known operational context, include it when it materially changes interpretation.

Examples:

- reduced publishing cadence,
- delayed asset production,
- campaign paused by design,
- seasonal closure,
- inventory limitation,
- client-approved testing period.

Do not use context as an excuse. Use it to explain scope and next action.

When the cause is shared between BOOST and the client, use collaborative language rather than blame.

Prefer:
`Hubo un hueco de producción y publicación. Ya estamos reforzando el flujo de material y edición para recuperar una cadencia estable.`

Avoid:
`El cliente tardó en mandar material.`

Avoid taking sole blame for a client-controlled dependency when that would be factually misleading.

## Recovery framing

A contained decline should normally be paired with a clear recovery action on the same slide or immediately afterward.

Useful structure:

`WHAT CHANGED → CONTEXT → WHAT WE ARE DOING`

Example:

`Las vistas por video en Instagram bajaron durante agosto. La caída coincide con un hueco de publicación y no se repitió igual en Facebook con los mismos videos. El siguiente foco es recuperar continuidad y medir cuatro semanas completas antes de cambiar la estrategia de contenido.`

## Do not repeat the problem

Once a negative signal has been established, do not restate it as a warning on the executive summary, platform slide, strategic-read slide, next-moves slide, watchlist and close unless each mention adds a new decision.

Repetition creates unnecessary alarm and makes the report feel worse than the data.

The core deck should normally state a contained issue once in detail, then refer to the action rather than repeating the problem.

## Internal risk vs client-facing relevance

Keep agency-operational diagnostics internal unless the client needs them to understand or approve a decision.

Examples that often belong in internal notes / appendix:

- exact campaign taxonomy,
- internal threshold alerts,
- creative-fatigue watch thresholds,
- follower-quality caveats that do not change a client action,
- detailed attribution mechanics,
- technical delivery anomalies that BOOST can resolve without client involvement.

Do not turn an internal optimization task into a client-facing warning simply because it is measurable.

## Confidence without false reassurance

Do not write `everything is fine`, `we are doing great`, `nothing to worry about`, or equivalent reassurance unless the evidence supports that claim.

Instead, show control through specificity:

- what improved,
- what softened,
- why that interpretation is reasonable,
- what BOOST will do next,
- what will be measured to confirm recovery.

Client confidence should come from clarity and ownership, not vague positivity.

## Account-health check before writing

Before drafting the client narrative, classify the period internally:

- `HEALTHY / OPTIMIZING` — overall objective is progressing; contained areas need improvement.
- `MIXED / NEEDS ATTENTION` — meaningful wins and meaningful declines coexist.
- `MATERIAL DECLINE` — core objective deteriorated enough that the report should communicate concern directly.

This internal classification controls tone. It must be evidence-based and should not appear as a client-facing score unless specifically designed for that account.

## Final framing QA

Before delivery, ask:

- Does the opening reflect the actual overall account health?
- Are verified wins easy to see?
- Did we give one contained decline disproportionate emotional weight?
- Is every material decline still visible and accurate?
- Did we imply causality where we only have correlation?
- Does every important issue have a clear next action?
- Did we repeat the same negative point unnecessarily?
- Did we expose internal technical risk that the client cannot act on?
- Does the report leave the client understanding both progress and the plan?

The standard is met when the client gets the truth, understands the context, and feels that BOOST knows what to do next.
